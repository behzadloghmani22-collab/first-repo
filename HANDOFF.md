## 0. READ THIS FIRST — the rule that governs everything below

> ### **If something can be done in nTop through nTopCL, doing it in anything else is unacceptable.**

This is the user's explicit instruction and it is not negotiable. It is the standard against
which the previous session's work **fails in several places**, and the main reason this
handoff exists.

nTop is the engineering authority. Python is a *launcher and a bookkeeper* — nothing more.
Python may:

- generate the list of parameter combinations to try (a DOE plan),
- write each combination into an nTopCL input JSON,
- **call `ntopcl.exe`**,
- read the numbers nTop returns,
- fit a surrogate, search it, and decide which combination to run in nTop next,
- draw charts and write the report.

Python may **NOT**:

- compute any physical or geometric property of the cage,
- estimate, approximate, extrapolate, scale, or otherwise invent an engineering quantity,
- substitute a closed-form formula for something nTop can evaluate,
- produce a number that is then presented as if nTop produced it.

**Every engineering quantity in the final results must be traceable to a value that came out
of an nTop Output block, in an `output.json` written by `ntopcl.exe`, on disk.** If a number
cannot be traced to such a file, it does not belong in the results.

## 3. Definition of "optimal" for a lumbar interbody fusion cage

These are the criteria the previous session derived and encoded. They are defensible and
should be kept as the *targets*; what must change is that **every one of them is measured
by nTop, not calculated in Python**. All thresholds live in `doe_config.json` and are meant
to be argued with, not treated as sacred.

The scoring method is a **Derringer–Suich desirability**: each criterion maps to a value
between 0 and 1 (1 = inside the clinical target window, 0 = beyond a hard limit), and the
criteria combine by a weighted geometric mean, so **violating any hard limit gives an
overall score of exactly zero**. The geometric mean is the right choice here because it
refuses to let a brilliant score on one axis compensate for a failure on another.

| # | Criterion | Target window (desirability = 1) | Hard limit (desirability = 0 outside) | Weight | Engineering rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | **Apparent axial modulus** | 2–5 GPa, target 3 GPa | 0.5–20 GPa | 0.30 | Solid Ti-6Al-4V is ~110 GPa; vertebral cancellous bone is ~0.1–2 GPa and cortical ~15–20 GPa. A stiff cage carries the load the bone should carry, the bone resorbs (stress shielding), and the cage subsides into the endplate. PEEK cages sit near 3.6 GPa and are the clinical benchmark. Matching that range while keeping titanium's osteo-integration is the entire point of a lattice cage. |
| C2 | **Porosity** | 60–75 % | 40–90 % | 0.22 | Open volume for bone in-growth and graft packing. Below ~40 % there is too little space for bone; above ~90 % the structure cannot carry load and cannot be printed reliably. |
| C3 | **Pore size** | 500–800 µm | 300–1000 µm | 0.22 | The window for *vascularised* bone in-growth. Below ~300 µm, in-growth is hypoxic and fibrous tissue forms instead of bone; above ~1000 µm, cells struggle to bridge and the specific surface collapses. |
| C4 | **Fatigue safety factor** at 1200 N cyclic | ≥ 2 | ≥ 1 | 0.12 | Walking / moderate daily activity at L4–L5. Assessed against an endurance limit of ~350 MPa for as-built LPBF Ti-6Al-4V (surface roughness dominates fatigue in printed lattices; do not use the wrought value). ASTM F2077 dynamic testing runs to 5 million cycles. |
| C5 | **Yield safety factor** at 2000 N peak | ≥ 4 | ≥ 2 | 0.06 | Lifting / worst-case static load. Yield ~950 MPa for heat-treated LPBF Ti-6Al-4V. ASTM F2077 static compression. |
| C6 | **Minimum realised strut / sheet thickness** | ≥ 0.45 mm | ≥ 0.30 mm | 0.04 | Laser powder-bed fusion cannot reliably resolve struts below ~0.3 mm; below that, struts come out undersized, rough, and with unpredictable fatigue behaviour. |
| C7 | **Mass** | lowest observed in the campaign | — | 0.02 | Tie-breaker only. |
| C8 | **Specific surface area** | highest observed in the campaign | — | 0.02 | Tie-breaker. More surface for osteo-conduction, within the pore-size constraint. |

### Criteria that SHOULD be added and that the previous session could not address

Add these once the nTop notebooks can measure them. They are real requirements, and the
previous session's omission of them is a gap, not a decision:

- **C9 — Powder evacuation / no closed cells.** Every void must connect to the exterior.
  A sealed pocket of un-sintered powder inside an implant is a hard fail, not a penalty.
  Treat it as a binary hard constraint.
- **C10 — Overhang self-support.** Down-facing surfaces below roughly 45° from the build
  plate need support that cannot be removed from inside a lattice. Report the worst
  overhang angle per design.
- **C11 — Endplate contact area.** Larger bearing area spreads load and lowers subsidence
  risk. Measure the real contact footprint, do not assume it.
- **C12 — Off-axis stiffness.** The cage sees flexion, extension, lateral bending and
  torsion, not pure axial compression. If homogenisation is used for C1 it yields the full
  stiffness tensor at no extra cost; report at least the axial and the two transverse moduli
  and flag strongly anisotropic designs.
- **C13 — Expulsion / shear resistance** per ASTM F2077, if the notebooks can be extended
  to that load case.

## 6. Practical notes for the local session

- **Licence contention:** nTopCL consumes a licence seat. Close the nTop GUI while a campaign
  runs, or the runs will fail with a licensing error. Keep `project.workers` at 1 unless the
  user knows they have multiple seats.
- **Run time is the binding constraint.** Each run is a real implicit-geometry build plus an
  FE solve. Measure it once and plan around it. Prefer fewer, better-chosen runs — that is
  exactly what the adaptive infill in `cage_doe/optimize.py` is for.
- **Failed runs are informative.** A lattice that fails to build (self-intersecting struts,
  or thickening into a solid block) marks an infeasible region. The driver already learns
  from these; do not discard them.
- **Resuming:** the campaign caches on disk. `--analyze-only` re-scores, re-optimises and
  redraws everything from cached nTop runs without launching nTop, which is the right way to
  explore different weightings once the expensive data exists.
- **Keep the machine awake** for the duration of a long campaign.
